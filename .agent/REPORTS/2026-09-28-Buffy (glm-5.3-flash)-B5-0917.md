## The finding: HumanSeatProbe is a flake generator

Three runs of the same class produced **three different check counts (37,
36, 37) and one outright failure (exit 1, "1 of 37")**. Mechanism, read off
the logs: the probe prints `Seed: 42` but takes no seed argument, and its
COVERAGE check — "sponsor/promote/build/lead/rotate/attack exercised" —
depends on the unseeded draw. The failing run's own tally shows
`promote=0 build=0 leadFleet=0 rotate=0 attack=0`: the random seat simply
never played those actions, so the coverage gate failed on a legal game.

**Disposition (recorded, not performed — row forbids it):** repair first
(seed argument + seeded coverage expectation, or reclassify the coverage
check as informational), *then* consider wiring. Wiring it today would add
a red herring to any gate.

## Reusable lesson

**A coverage gate on a random seat is a flake generator**: any check that
asserts "the random game exercised X" fails lawfully whenever the draw
doesn't exercise X — and a probe that prints a seed but accepts no seed
argument cannot be reproduces or trusted in a gate. Seed the harness, or
demote the coverage assertion to information.
