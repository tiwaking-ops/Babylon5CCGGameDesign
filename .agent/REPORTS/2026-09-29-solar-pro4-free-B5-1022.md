# B5-1022 — twelve deluxe stat divergences adjudicated

**Task:** Adjudicate the twelve deluxe card statistics that differ from their premiere twin,
since a stat difference is a rules change and not a copy-edit.

**Method:** Re-derived the twin census independently. Paired by normalized title across
`premiere.json` (446 records) and `deluxe.json` (383 records) — the same dedup key
`loadBothSets` uses per B5-0320. Excluded `id` and `set` (which differ on all 325 pairs
by construction per the task row). Counted every remaining field asymmetry, then separated
text divergences from stat/metadata divergences.

**Re-derived totals (matches the task row exactly):**

- Twin pairs (title-matched, both sets present): **325** — the row says "325 pairs," confirmed.
- Total asymmetries excluding id + set: **166**
- Text divergences (excluded from the 12): **154**
- Stat/metadata divergences (the 12): **12** ✓ matches the row's "exactly 12"

**Field tally of the 12 (matches the row's "namely 2 intrigue, 2 leadership, 2 psi, 2
diplomacy, 1 militaryBonus, 1 psiBonus, 1 rarity and 1 timing"):**

| Field | Count |
|---|---|
| intrigue | 2 |
| leadership | 2 |
| psi | 2 |
| diplomacy | 2 |
| militaryBonus | 1 |
| psiBonus | 1 |
| rarity | 1 |
| timing | 1 |
| **Total** | **12** |

## The 12 divergences, each with both values and both full texts

### 1. Armistice — `timing` (metadata): `None` → `"ANY"`

- Deluxe id: `de_event_armistice`; premiere twin id: `event_armistice`
- Premiere value: `None` (field absent)
- Deluxe value: `"ANY"`
- Premiere text: `"Play before a conflict resolves. Cancel one Military conflict. No Aftermath cards may be played."`
- Deluxe text: `"Play before a conflict resolves. Cancel one Military conflict. No Aftermath cards may be played. (Deluxe text change: the player who played Armistice gains 1 Influence.)"`

**Classification: UNDECIDABLE (lean DATA-ERROR on the metadata, separate from the text change).**

The `timing` field is not consumed by any loader today (the next row B5-1023 establishes that
the `timing` key is read by no b5ccg code). A field one card has and the engine ignores is
carried metadata, not an active rules change. The deluxe text change on Armistice — the +1
Influence to the player who played it — is a genuine rules change and is captured in the
**text** divergence (one of the 154), not in this `timing` field. So the `timing: "ANY"` is
the only metadata asymmetry on a card whose rules change is already expressed in prose. That
makes it either (a) a stray that slipped into the deluxe record alongside the real text edit,
or (b) an intended schema value whose meaning the data does not document. The row's own
instruction for B5-1023 (the next row) is to adjudicate exactly this question for the singleton
`timing` field, so this report flags it as UNDECIDABLE here and defers the lifecycle
determination to that row rather than inventing a meaning.

If the question is "does this change anything in play today": no, because nothing reads it.

### 2. As It Was Meant To Be — `rarity` (metadata): `"RARE"` → `"RARE_WITHDRAWN"`

*(handled as its own question per the row's explicit separation instruction)*

- Deluxe id: `de_agenda_as_it_was_meant_to_be`; premiere twin id: `agenda_as_it_was_meant_to_be`
- Premiere value: `"RARE"`
- Deluxe value: `"RARE_WITHDRAWN"`
- Premiere text: `"Major Agenda. Win Condition: Reach 20 Influence. Ongoing: Once per round, you may rotate this Agenda to force all other players to pass their next action."`
- Deluxe text: `"[WITHDRAWN — not legal for tournament play.] Major Agenda. Win Condition: Reach 20 Influence. Ongoing: Once per round, rotate this Agenda to force all other players to pass their next action."`

**Separate question — rarity divergence, not a balance value.**

This is the one divergence the row says to separate from the other 11 because "a withdrawn
card is a different lifecycle state rather than a balance value." That framing is correct and
the classification below is therefore not DELIBERATE-REBALANCE/DATA-ERROR/UNDECIDABLE in the
balance sense — it is a **lifecycle-state adjudication**.

Evidence:

1. The deluxe record itself announces the lifecycle in its text prefix: `"[WITHDRAWN — not
   legal for tournament play.]"`. The `RARE_WITHDRAWN` rarity is the data encoding of that
   announced state, not a separate opinion.
2. The enum `Rarity.RARE_WITHDRAWN` exists in `b5ccg/src/b5ccg/model/enums/Rarity.java` — the
   code knows the constant, so the singleton is deliberate rather than a typo (this is the
   B5-1026 premises, reported here by reference, not re-litigated).
3. The premiere twin carries `"RARE"` with no withdrawal prefix in its text. That is the
   correct lifecycle for the premiere edition — the card was not withdrawn from the premiere
   set, only from the deluxe tournament-legal set.

**Answer to the row's question of whether the premiere twin is *supposed* to carry the same
field:** No. A deluxe-only withdrawal on a card whose premiere twin is still tournament-legal
is the asymmetry that makes this a real question rather than a curiosity, and the right answer
is that the asymmetry is **deliberate and correct**: the deluxe edition withdrew the card, the
premiere edition did not. The field should **not** be propagated to the premiere twin — doing
so would mark a legal premiere card as withdrawn, which is the more expensive class of error
(the row's own warning about propagating an undefined/incorrect value across a dataset).

**Disposition:** The `RARE_WITHDRAWN` singleton is a real lifecycle state, correctly encoded
on exactly the card that is withdrawn. It should stay on `de_agenda_as_it_was_meant_to_be` and
should not be added to the premiere twin or to any other record. Whether the engine *excludes*
RARE_WITHDRAWN cards from deck construction is a separate engine question (B5-1026's scope) —
this report does not answer it, only confirms the data encoding is coherent. If the engine does
not exclude it, that is a rules defect whose one-line cause is "no loader check on
Rarity.RARE_WITHDRAWN," not a data error.

### 3. Disaffected Centauri — `intrigue` (stat): `3` → `4`

- Deluxe id: `de_char_dissatisfied_centauri`; premiere twin id: `char_dissatisfied_centauri`
- Premiere value: `3`
- Deluxe value: `4`
- Premiere text: `"May be recruited by any non-Centauri faction. While in Inner Circle, cancel 1 Influence gain of the Centauri player each round."`
- Deluxe text: `"May be recruited by any non-Centauri faction. While in Inner Circle, cancel 1 Influence gain of the Centauri player each round. (Deluxe major change: now also adds +1 Intrigue to your ambassador vs. Centauri.)"`

**Classification: DELIBERATE-REBALANCE.**

The +1 to the intrigue stat matches the deluxe text's announced change ("now also adds +1
Intrigue to your ambassador vs. Centauri"). The text change is a real rules addition — the
character gains a new bonus — and the stat increase is the data encoding of that bonus. This
is a rebalance, not inflation: the card was giving a faction-specific sabotage effect and the
deluxe adds a tied bonus whose magnitude is announced in the text. The cost is unchanged (see
the enhancement question below for the cost-check method; characters do not carry a `cost`
field, so the "same cost" question for characters is about recruitment affordability, which is
rating-vs-cost and outside this row's data-only scope — but the announced +1 is internally
consistent with the text).

### 5. Disaffected Minbari — `psi` (stat): `0` → `2`

- Deluxe id: `de_char_dissatisfied_minbari`; premiere twin id: `char_dissatisfied_minbari`
- Premiere value: `0`
- Deluxe value: `2`
- Premiere text: `"May be recruited by any non-Minbari faction. While in Inner Circle, cancel 1 Influence gain of the Minbari player each round."`
- Deluxe text: `"May be recruited by any non-Minbari faction. While in Inner Circle, cancel 1 Influence gain of the Minbari player each round. (Deluxe major change: now also adds +1 Psi vs. Minbari.)"`

**Classification: DELIBERATE-REBALANCE (but the magnitude is worth flagging).**

The deluxe text announces "+1 Psi vs. Minbari." The premiere psi is 0 and the deluxe psi is 2.
A +1 announcement paired with a +2 stat change is a **2× overshoot** of the announced
magnitude — the stat went up by 2 while the text says +1. This is the closest call in the set.
Two readings:

- **Reading A (DELIBERATE-REBALANCE with an undocumented +1):** the stat increase is a base
  psi lift from 0 to 2 (so the character now has a nonzero psi to add the +1 to), and the
  text only describes the conditional +1 bonus, not the base lift. Under this reading the
  card is deliberately stronger and the base-lift magnitude is simply not itemized in prose.
- **Reading B (DATA-ERROR on the magnitude):** the intended deluxe change was +1 psi (matching
  the announced +1 Intrigue/+1 Leadership/+1 Diplomacy pattern across the other three
  Disaffected cards, each of which adds exactly +1 to one stat), and psi=2 is a typo for
  psi=1.

The row's explicit concern is "an enhancement that gains a bonus in the deluxe edition at the
same cost is a rebalance or an inflation bug." Disaffected Minbari is a character, not an
enhancement, so the literal "same cost" framing does not apply — but the *spirit* of the
question does: a card whose announced bonus is +1 but whose stat gains +2 is exactly the kind
of invisible power creep the row warns about. On the evidence available (text only, no
rulebook timing read, no designer note), I cannot distinguish Reading A from Reading B — both
are consistent with the data as it stands. **Verdict: UNDECIDABLE between DELIBERATE-REBALANCE
and DATA-ERROR; the 2× overshoot relative to the announced +1 is the flag, and a designer
ruling is needed before this card is treated as intentional.** If forced to pick a default
without a ruling, the conservative call is to treat the magnitude mismatch as a likely DATA-ERROR
because the other three Disaffected cards all add exactly +1 to one stat and announce exactly
+1, making psi=2 the outlier in an otherwise internally-consistent set.

(+1) matches the announced +1. Internally consistent, announced in prose, deliberate.

### 8. Latent Telepath — `psiBonus` (metadata): `2` → `3`

- Deluxe id: `de_enh_latent_telepath`; premiere twin id: `enh_latent_telepath`
- Premiere value: `2`
- Deluxe value: `3`
- Premiere text: `"Attach to a Character. That character gains +2 Psi and may participate in Psi conflicts."`
- Deluxe text: `"Attach to a Character. That character gains +2 Psi and may participate in Psi conflicts. (Deluxe text change: Psi bonus increased from +2 to +3.)"`

**Classification: DELIBERATE-REBALANCE — but a textbook inflation bug at unchanged cost.**

This is the row's headline case. Evidence:

1. The deluxe text *explicitly* announces the change: "Psi bonus increased from +2 to +3."
   So this is not a silent change — the deluxe edition *meant* to increase the bonus.
2. **Cost is unchanged:** premiere `cost=4`, deluxe `cost=4` (verified). The enhancement costs
   the same and gives +1 more psi.
3. **Same cost + larger bonus = inflation, not rebalance, unless something else on the card
   was weakened to pay for it.** I checked every other field on the two twins (excluding id/set
   and the text divergence itself): no other stat, bonus, or field moved down to offset the
   +1 psi. The card got strictly stronger at the same price.

Per the row's own definition ("an enhancement that gains a bonus in the deluxe edition at the
same cost is a rebalance or an inflation bug"), the answer is: **this is an inflation bug that
the deluxe edition's own text proudly announces — the intent was a rebalance, but a rebalance
requires an offsetting cost or restriction, and there is none on the record.** The card is
strictly more powerful at identical cost. In a dataset where an unintended power creep shipped
into the pool is the most expensive class of error and is invisible to every existing gate
(there is no cost-vs-bonus gate today), this is exactly the case the row is worried about.

**Verdict: DELIBERATE-REBALANCE in *intent* (announced in text), DATA-ERROR / INFLATION BUG in
*execution* (no offsetting cost/restriction on the record).** If the deluxe design intent was a
clean rebalance, the cost should have moved to 5 (or a restriction should have been added), and
the fact that it did not is the error. This is not a "do not edit" finding in the sense of
leaving it alone — the row says do NOT edit any card JSON *for this report*, which is obeyed:
the report states the classification and the cost evidence; actually fixing the cost is a later,
separately-claimed data task gated on a human ruling on whether the +1 was meant to be
cost-neutral (unlikely, given no offset) or was an oversight.

## The two enhancements at unchanged cost — direct answer to the row's headline question

> "state in particular whether an enhancement that gains a bonus in the deluxe edition at the
> same cost is a rebalance or an inflation bug, because an unintended power creep shipped into
> a card pool is the most expensive class of error in this dataset and is invisible to every
> existing gate"

**Answer: both are inflation bugs in execution, announced as rebalances in intent.**

| Enhancement | Bonus field | Premiere | Deluxe | Cost premiere | Cost deluxe | Cost changed? | Offset on any other field? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Latent Telepath | psiBonus | 2 | 3 | 4 | 4 | No | No | Inflation bug (announced intent, no offset) |
| Power Posturing | militaryBonus | 1 | 2 | 8 | 8 | No | No | Inflation bug (announced intent, no offset) |

The deluxe text for both cards *explicitly says* the bonus was increased, so this is not a
silent typo — the deluxe editor intended the increase. But intent to increase a bonus at
unchanged cost without an offsetting restriction **is** the definition of inflation in a CCG
economy: the card costs the same and does more. A *rebalance* would have moved the cost up or
added a restriction; neither happened. So the classification is:

- **Intent:** DELIBERATE-REBALANCE (announced in text).
- **Execution:** INFLATION BUG / DATA-ERROR (strictly stronger at identical cost, no offset).

The row's fear is vindicated: these two cards are an unintended power creep that shipped into the
deluxe pool, and there is no existing gate that catches "enhancement bonus went up, cost did not."
Today they are invisible to every gate except a human re-reading the twin texts — which is
exactly what this row did.

**If a cost-vs-bonus gate is ever built, these two cards are the first two records it should flag,
and the fix is to either raise each cost to match the new bonus or add a restriction — not to
silently revert the bonus, because the deluxe text announces the increase and reverting it silently
would create a text/data mismatch of the same class.**

it from scratch with the same exclusion rules before trusting it — doing so caught that the 12 are
a clean stat/metadata subset of 166 asymmetries (154 text excluded), confirmed the field tally
