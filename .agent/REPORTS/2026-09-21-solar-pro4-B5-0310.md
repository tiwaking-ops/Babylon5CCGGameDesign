# B5-0310 report — UI playability audit (read-only, report-only)

## 1. What I did

Read-only audit of `b5ccg/src/b5ccg/ui/` (3 files:
`MainWindow.java`, `HandPanel.java`, `GameBoardPanel.java`) against the
canonical rulebook `BABYLON5_CCG_RULEBOOK.md` (sections I, II, III, IV, V) and
the live model/engine surface the UI is wired to (`GameState`, `Player`,
`GamePhase`, `GameController.submitHumanAction`/`isWaitingForHuman`,
`ConflictCard`).

No `ui/` or any other `b5ccg/src/` file was modified. This is a report file
only, as the task specifies.

Audit criteria: does the UI let a human player perform the actions the
rulebook permits during a turn, and does it give the human enough information
to do so without misreading the state? I ranked each finding by severity.

## 2. Read-only surface consulted

UI (3 files, all read-only):

- `MainWindow.java` — JFrame shell, toolbar (Pass + "Play / Initiate"),
  log scroll, `onStateUpdate`/`refresh` gating, `playSelected()` human action
  path.
- `HandPanel.java` — card list, click-to-select, `CardSelectedListener`
  callback, `paintComponent` rendering (title/type/faction/stats/abbreviated
  text/rarity dot/index).
- `GameBoardPanel.java` — per-player zone rendering (ambassador, Inner
  Circle, fleets, groups/locations, deck/hand counts, agenda), active-conflict
  banner, phase tag.

Model/engine surface read for wiring context (no UI edit):

- `b5ccg/src/b5ccg/model/Player.java` — `getInfluence()` returns the
  **Influence Rating**, not available-to-spend this turn (line 36). Turn
  spendable pool is not a separate field; the rulebook's "apply up to your
  Influence Rating" is enforced model-side in the action handlers, not in the
  UI.
- `b5ccg/src/b5ccg/model/GameState.java` — exposes `GamePhase` only (line 40);
  there is **no sub-step tracking** (e.g. "Conflict round Step 1/2" or
  "Action round: my turn / not yet my turn") that the UI could key on.
- `b5ccg/src/b5ccg/model/enums/GamePhase.java` — 6 values:
  `SETUP, ACTION, CONFLICT_RESOLUTION, AFTERMATH, DRAW, END_ROUND`.
- `b5ccg/src/b5ccg/engine/GameController.java` — human sync is
  `isWaitingForHuman` (line 286): the engine sets `waitingForHuman = true`
  and notifies the UI; the human submits via `submitHumanAction(action)`;
  the engine does **not** gate on phase — any `GameAction` the human submits
  is accepted if the engine's current step is waiting for a human.
- `b5ccg/src/b5ccg/model/ConflictCard.java` — `getConflictType()`,
  `getInfluenceReward()`, no target field (target is chosen at initiation).

## 3. Findings (ranked by severity)

### P0 — Human cannot perform rulebook actions at all from the UI

These are gaps where the rulebook provides a legal action and the UI offers no
path to it. The human is not playing the full game; they are watching the AI
play and pressing Pass or "Play / Initiate" on one card.

**F1 (P0/MEDIUM) — No support / oppose UI during an active conflict.**

Rulebook §IV "Conflict Cards" + §V "ACTION: Support or Oppose a Conflict":
during the Action round, characters and fleets may rotate to support or oppose
any conflict currently in play; each participant adds its ability to the
conflict's support or opposition total, and resolution depends on those totals
(rulebook §III Resolution Round Step 1: "add up the total ability applied in
support and compare it with the total applied in opposition").

In the code: `GameBoardPanel.paintComponent` draws an active-conflict banner
(lines 42-52: "CONFLICT: <title> [<type>]") but provides no way for the human
to join. There is no "Support" / "Oppose" control, no participant list, no
support-vs-opposition total display. The AI joins automatically inside
`GameController.resolveCurrentConflict()` (AI loop calls `ai.shouldJoinConflict`
per participant), but the human has no UI path.

Impact: the human cannot participate in any conflict they did not initiate,
and cannot contribute support/opposition to any conflict at all. In a
single-human vs AI game this means the human's conflict participation is
limited to whatever the AI auto-decides — the human is effectively absent from
the resolution step of every conflict.

Rulebook reference: §III "The Action Round", §IV "Conflict Cards" (support /
oppose), §V "ACTION: Support or Oppose a Conflict", §III "The Resolution
Round" Step 1.

**F2 (P0/MEDIUM) — No target selection for initiated conflicts; auto-target only.**

Rulebook §III "The Conflict Round" Step 2 "Reveal and Initiate Conflicts":
"any further required details (such as the target of the conflict) must be
declared at this time." Several conflict cards target a specific player, fleet,
location, or character; even unconstrained conflicts need a declared target.

In the code: `MainWindow.playSelected()` (lines 131-152) handles
`instanceof ConflictCard` by auto-targeting the leading non-human player
(lines 136-144: highest `getInfluence()` among non-human players), with no UI
for the human to choose. The human cannot target a specific opponent, a
location, or a character.

Impact: the human cannot deliberately target a weak opponent, a specific
location, or avoid targeting the strongest opponent. Conflicts are resolved in
initiative order and targeting matters for aftermath eligibility and conflict
text, so this narrows the human's strategic options to whatever the auto-target
picks.

Rulebook reference: §III "The Conflict Round" Step 2, §IV "Conflict Cards".

**F3 (P0/HIGH) — Most rulebook Action-round actions are unreachable from the UI.**

Rulebook §V "Action Details" lists at least 14 distinct action types a player
may take during the Action round: sponsor a supporting card, promote a
character to the Inner Circle, rotate to build influence, use an effect that
requires rotating a card, lead a fleet, support or oppose a conflict, attack a
conflict participant, play an event or contingency, discard/replace/reveal/
sponsor an agenda, heal a character, repair a fleet or location, offer
influence to a mercenary, and pass.

In the code: the human UI exposes exactly two actions — Pass and "Play /
Initiate" (which maps to `playSelected()`, i.e. play a card from hand or
initiate a conflict with a conflict card). There is no UI for:
sponsor/promote/build-influence/lead-fleet/support-oppose/attack/play-event/
play-contingency/discard-replace-reveal-sponsor-agenda/heal/repair/offer-
influence-to-mercenary. The human cannot rotate an in-play Inner Circle
character to build influence, cannot sponsor a card from hand (unless the only
mechanism is "Play / Initiate" on a non-conflict card, which `playSelected`
maps to `GameAction.playCard` — but there is no cost gate, no Inner Circle
rotation step, and no support-row placement visible), cannot lead a fleet, etc.

Impact: this is the single biggest playability gap. The human is not playing
the Action round; they are performing one action (play a card or initiate a
conflict) and passing. The rulebook Action round is a multi-action, multi-step,
initiative-ordered round; the UI reduces it to a single card play. For a
single-human game this is a structural limitation of the UI, not a cosmetic
issue.

Rulebook reference: §III "The Action Round", §V "Action Details" (all
subsections).

### P1 — Misleading or ambiguous UI (the human can act, but may misunderstand what they're doing)

**F4 (P1/MEDIUM) — "Play / Initiate" button conflates two distinct rulebook actions.**

Rulebook §V treats "play a card" (event, contingency, enhancement, etc.) and
"initiate a conflict" as separate actions with different timing, costs, and
conditions. The UI button label "Play / Initiate" collapses both into one
control; the human selects a card and the code branches on `instanceof
ConflictCard` (lines 134-148).

Impact: the label does not tell the human which action will be taken until they
have already selected a card. A human who selects a conflict card expecting to
"play" it as an event-like card will instead initiate a conflict; a human who
selects a non-conflict card expecting to "initiate" something will instead play
it. The ambiguity is mild but real for a new player.

Rulebook reference: §V "Action Details" (different actions, different rules).

**F5 (P1/HIGH) — No cost / affordability feedback before the human plays a card.**

Rulebook §V "ACTION: Sponsor a Supporting Card" and §V "ACTION: Promote a
Character to the Inner Circle": both require applying the listed influence cost
(and double cost for other-race characters; +1 per Inner Circle member for
promotion). §V "ACTION: Rotate to Build Influence": costs 3 influence. The
human must know whether they can afford an action before taking it.

In the code: `GameBoardPanel.drawZone` shows `Influence: <rating>` (line 71),
and `MainWindow.refresh` shows the active player, but there is no display of
what playing the currently selected card would cost, whether the human can
afford it, what the card's play conditions are, or what the result would be.
`playSelected()` submits the action without any pre-play affordability or
condition check visible to the human. The model enforces costs in the action
handlers (e.g. `RulesEngine`/`GameController`), but the UI gives the human no
preview.

Impact: the human can attempt to play a card they cannot afford, or play a card
whose conditions are not met, and only find out by seeing the log/result after
submission. For a new player this is a real source of confusion and wasted
actions.

Rulebook reference: §V "ACTION: Sponsor a Supporting Card", §V "ACTION:
Promote a Character to the Inner Circle", §V "ACTION: Rotate to Build
Influence", §IV "Anatomy of a Card" item 2 (Influence Cost).

**F6 (P1/MEDIUM) — No phase-aware action gating beyond "my turn".**

Rulebook §III "Playing the Game" / "The Action Round": the Action round has a
specific structure (initiative order, one action at a time, pass to end the
round after consecutive passes). The human's UI gating in `MainWindow.refresh`
(lines 114-116) enables `passButton` and `playCardButton` when
`myTurn` = `state.getActivePlayer() == human && controller.isWaitingForHuman()`.
That is a phase-agnostic "is it my turn and is the engine waiting for me"
gate — it does not distinguish the Action round from the Conflict round, the
Draw round, or the aftermath step, and it does not tell the human which actions
are valid in the current phase.

Impact: the human cannot tell from the UI what they are allowed to do right now
beyond "my turn / not my turn". In particular, the human cannot tell whether
the current phase is one where supporting/opposing a conflict is allowed, where
building influence is allowed, where aftermaths can be played, etc. The UI
presents the same two buttons regardless.

Rulebook reference: §III "Playing the Game" (round structure), §III "The Action
Round", §III "The Resolution Round", §III "The Draw Round".

### P2 — Information deficits (the human lacks data to make informed decisions)

**F7 (P2/MEDIUM) — No conflict participation visualization.**

When a conflict is active, `GameBoardPanel` draws a banner with the conflict
title and type (lines 42-52) but does not show:
- which characters/fleets have been committed to support or oppose,
- the current support vs opposition totals,
- which player (including the human) has joined which side.

The human sees that a conflict exists but not its state. The rulebook resolution
depends on those totals (§III Resolution Round Step 1), and aftermath eligibility
depends on who participated and who won/lost (§IV Aftermath Cards, §V "ACTION:
Support or Oppose a Conflict").

Impact: the human cannot make informed decisions about whether to join a
conflict (if the UI ever supports it — see F1) or about what aftermaths they
might be eligible to play.

Rulebook reference: §III "The Resolution Round" Step 1, §IV "Conflict Cards"
(support/oppose), §IV "Aftermath Cards" (Participant conditions).

**F8 (P2/LOW) — No initiative order display.**

Rulebook §III "The Ready Round" Step 3 "Determine Initiative" + §III "The
Action Round": initiative order is determined by Influence Rating (lowest acts
first), with ambassador ability tiebreaks (Diplomacy, Intrigue, Psi, Leadership
in that order). The human's position in initiative order affects when they act
and therefore what they see other players do before acting.

In the code: `GameBoardPanel` highlights the active player's zone (lines 56-62)
and shows the phase tag for the active player (lines 75-78), but does not show
the full initiative order for the turn. The human cannot tell from the UI where
they sit relative to the other players.

Impact: mild confusion for a new player about why other players are acting
before or after them. Not a blocking issue, but a missing piece of context the
rulebook explicitly says matters.

Rulebook reference: §III "The Ready Round" Step 3, §III "The Action Round".

**F9 (P2/LOW) — No conflict-type-to-ability legend.**

Rulebook §IV "Type of Conflict": four conflict types (Diplomatic, Intrigue,
Military, Psi), each resolved using the corresponding ability (Diplomacy,
Intrigue, Military, Psi). §V "ACTION: Support or Oppose a Conflict": "Only
cards with a non-zero ability of the appropriate type may rotate to support or
oppose a conflict."

In the code: `HandPanel.drawCard` colors conflict cards red and shows the type
and influence reward (lines 124-127: e.g. "MILITARY  +3 INF"), and
`GameBoardPanel`'s conflict banner shows the type as text (line 48-49). There
is no legend or explanation that maps conflict types to the resolving abilities,
so a new player cannot tell that a Military conflict is resolved with Military
ability, that characters don't have Military, or that only fleets (and
Leadership-leading characters) can participate in a Military conflict.

Impact: a new player seeing a "MILITARY" conflict banner will not know what
they can contribute to it (if the UI ever allows joining — see F1). This is a
teaching/legend gap, not a functional bug.

Rulebook reference: §IV "Type of Conflict", §IV "Conflict Cards", §V "ACTION:
Support or Oppose a Conflict" (non-zero ability of the appropriate type).

**F10 (P2/LOW) — Ambassador's assistant effect not surfaced.**

Rulebook §IV "Your Ambassador's Assistant": the assistant can rotate to give
the ambassador +1 Diplomacy/Intrigue/Leadership while rotated, or allow the
ambassador to apply 1 less influence when sponsoring later that turn. The
assistant is an Inner Circle character with its own abilities and can act
independently.

In the code: `GameBoardPanel.drawZone` draws the assistant as a mini card in the
Inner Circle (lines 82-96) but provides no status indicator for whether it is
rotated, no UI to rotate it, and no text or icon indicating its +1 ability bonus
or reduced-sponsorship cost effect. The human cannot see whether the assistant
is currently rotated and contributing.

Impact: the human cannot tell from the UI whether their assistant is rotated and
actively boosting the ambassador; they would have to infer it from the game
state or the log. Mild information deficit.

Rulebook reference: §IV "Your Ambassador's Assistant".

### P3 — Polish / scale issues (functional but degrades with scale or is just rough)

**F11 (P3/LOW) — Zone rendering overflow without warning.**

`GameBoardPanel.drawZone` lays out Inner Circle (line 92-96), fleets (103-107),
and groups/locations (114-123) in horizontal rows with a hard break when
`cx + 50 > x + w - 4`. If a player has more Inner Circle characters, fleets, or
groups/locations than fit, the later cards simply do not render — there is no
scroll, no pagination, and no "N more" indicator. With 4+ Inner Circle
characters or a fleshed-out fleet/Group/Location collection the human will miss
cards.

Impact: at small scale (typical early game) this is fine; at scale it silently
hides the human's own cards. Not a rulebook violation, but a playability
degradation.

**F12 (P3/LOW) — Log is a raw dump, not a structured narrative.**

Rulebook §III "Narrating the Story": players are encouraged to narrate actions
and tell the story. `MainWindow.refresh` (lines 108-112) dumps the raw state
log into the `JTextArea` with no narrative framing, timestamps, or grouping by
round/phase. The log is usable as a trace but does not read as a story; for a
new player it is functional but not friendly.

Impact: cosmetic / onboarding issue, not a functional gap. The log is there and
accurate; it's just not written for a human reader.

Rulebook reference: §III "Narrating the Story".

**F13 (P3/LOW) — Hand card rendering omits influence cost.**

`HandPanel.drawCard` shows title, type, faction, stats (for characters: D/I/P/L;
for fleets: MIL; for conflicts: type + reward), abbreviated text, rarity dot,
and index — but not the card's influence cost. The rulebook §IV "Anatomy of a
Card" item 2 lists Influence Cost as a primary card attribute, and several
actions (sponsor, promote, build influence) are cost-gated. Without the cost on
the card face, the human must remember or look up costs.

Impact: mild — the human can still read the abbreviated text, and the model
enforces costs on play, but the cost is a key piece of card anatomy that the UI
omits. Relevant primarily if the UI ever expands to expose sponsor/promote/
build-influence actions (which would make cost preview critical — see F5).

Rulebook reference: §IV "Anatomy of a Card" item 2 (Influence Cost).

## 4. What is NOT a defect (by design / acceptable simplification)

- **Single-human assumption.** `MainWindow` title is "Babylon 5 CCG — Single
  Player" and the constructor takes one `GameController` that internally drives
  AI players. This matches the smoke test's 4-AI-player setup. A single-human
  UI is a deliberate scope choice, not a defect.
- **Auto-targeting as a default.** `playSelected()` auto-targets the leading
  non-human player. This is a simplification, not a bug, but it is exactly the
  gap F2 reports — the simplification is the finding.
- **"Play / Initiate" as a single action path.** Collapsing the Action round to
  one card play is the structural gap F3 reports; it is the design the UI
  currently has, and the audit flags it as a playability limitation.
- **Phase-agnostic human gating.** The engine's `isWaitingForHuman` gate is
  phase-agnostic by design (the engine decides when to wait for the human). The
  audit reports the UI's lack of phase context as F6, not the engine's gating.

Open questions for a follow-up (out of scope for this report, which is
read-only):

- Whether the project intends the human UI to eventually expose the full
  Action-round action set (sponsor / promote / build influence / lead fleet /
  support-oppose / attack / event / contingency / agenda / heal / repair /
  mercenary) — if yes, F3, F1, F2, F5, F6, F7, F9, F10 all become larger
  tasks and F5 (cost preview) becomes critical-path.
- Whether the human is intended to be able to join conflicts at all — if yes,
  F1 and F2 are the core gaps, and F7 (participation visualization) is the
  necessary companion.
- Whether the UI is intended for onboarding/new players or only for the AI study
  harness — if the latter, the severity of F4-F13 drops substantially and only
  F1/F2/F3 remain as structural gaps relative to the rulebook.
