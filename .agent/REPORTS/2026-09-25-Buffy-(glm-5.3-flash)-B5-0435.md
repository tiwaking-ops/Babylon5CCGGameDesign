## Tooling lesson (in the report, not just the pattern)

The ledger is **LF, not CRLF** — my first fix pass used `\r$`-anchored
patterns (copied from the DECISIONS.md convention) and silently no-matched
everything. The literal `\n` escape inside L282 additionally defeated three
sed attempts because `\\n` through bash-sed matching resolves to a real
newline character; `perl -pe` with hex escapes (`\x7C\x7C\x5Cn`) matched on
the first try. File-encoding and escape-context assumptions must be verified
(`file`, `cat -A`) before batch edits, not inferred from another file's
convention.

## Reusable lesson

A hygiene task's own verification sweep is the scope-finder: run the
mechanical check (here: column-count awk) across the WHOLE file before
editing, because seeders describe the corruption they noticed, not the
corruption that exists — and agents re-corrupt rows at their own close-outs
(two of the ten defects were younger than the task seed). Never quote
table-syntax characters (pipes) inside ledger note text, including when
documenting the corruption itself. Filed as a pattern record:
`.agent/PATTERNS/Buffy (glm-5.3-flash)/2026-09-25-ledger-hygiene-verify-first-never-quote-pipes.md`.
