## What makes this gate more than a status check

B5-0947's `BLOCKED` note records **two** independent blockers, and the second one
means a plain `OPEN → DONE` transition would **not** be enough:

1. **Conjunction gate red at the time.** It demanded eight predecessor batches all
   `DONE`; `B5-0939` (AFTERMATH, 58 titles) was `OPEN` with no report anywhere.
   **This blocker has since cleared — B5-0939 now reads `DONE`.**
2. **The premise is withdrawn.** B5-0947 asks to rule whether the pool stays the
   authored design layer *"per B5-0654"*, but per the **B5-0821** `DECISIONS`
   entry B5-0654 is **WITHDRAWN by human ruling with no replacement stated**. So
   B5-0947 requests a ruling whose stated authority no longer exists, and
   clearing blocker 1 does not make it runnable. **This needs a human decision,
   recorded and not pre-empted by me.**

So the honest statement of this row's gate is not "wait for B5-0947". It is:

> **For B5-0961 to become executable, a human must first rule which layer wins**
> (B5-0654 ruling: the authored pool, or the printed/advisory text), **restate the
> authority B5-0947 will rule under** to replace the withdrawn B5-0654, and only
> then can B5-0947 reach `DONE` — at which point B5-0961's stated precondition is
> satisfied and the backfill plan becomes writable.

I am **not** deciding the B5-0947 question, not choosing a layer, and not writing

## Reusable lesson

> A row that gates itself on an upstream task's *status* can hide a deeper gate
> behind that status: before treating `BLOCKED upstream` as merely "not finished
> yet", read the upstream row's **note** for blockers that no status transition
> will clear — a withdrawn ruling, or a premise needing a human — because those
> make the downstream gate permanently red and re-measuring the status alone will
> never turn it green.
