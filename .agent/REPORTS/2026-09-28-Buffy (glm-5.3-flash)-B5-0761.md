## What the contract is, and where it was asserted

The B5-0679 surrender contract is defined at two places in `AIPlayer`:

1. **The OFFER gate** (`buildLegalActions`): when `difficulty != EASY` and the
   player trails the leader by `>= 6` influence, offer `SURRENDER` for every
   target where `rules.canSurrender(p, target, state)` holds.
2. **The SCORE band** (`scoreSurrenderMedium` / `scoreSurrenderHard`): −1 below
   threshold; `10 − gap` (MEDIUM) / `5 − gap·0.5` (HARD) above it, plus the
   B5-0727 unrest-pressure band and the civil-surrender discount; feed-the-
   leader penalties at the target level.

Both were asserted **directly** (the offer list via reflection on
`buildLegalActions`; scores via reflection on the scorers), with EASY
uniformity sampled 300× through the public `chooseAction` window.

## Probe-design findings (the two failures that taught)

1. **v1 inferred the gate from the CHOSEN action — wrong level.** All negative
   gates passed but every positive case "failed": at gap 7 the fixture's legal
   list also contained `DECLARE_WAR_CONFLICT` against the same at-war target,
   scored 5 (MEDIUM) vs surrender's 3, so the argmax correctly picked war.
   The offer gate and the pick are different contracts; assert the gate at
   the gate.
2. **v2 compared picks to another invocation's legal list by reference —
   always false.** `buildLegalActions` constructs fresh `GameAction` objects
   per call (measured 300/300 "outside"); the comparison is structural
   (type + target + card).

## Reusable lesson

Assert a gate at the gate, not through the argmax: a chooser's pick is the
maximum over the whole legal set, so any legal competitor that out-scores the
action under test will mask a perfectly healthy offer — and a probe that
"fails" there would send the next agent hunting for a defect in correct code.
