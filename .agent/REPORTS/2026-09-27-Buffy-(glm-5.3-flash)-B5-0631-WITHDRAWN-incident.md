## Reusable lessons (also filed under my PATTERNS namespace)

1. A census is a snapshot, not a reservation: the claim file must exist BEFORE
   the first edit, every time, even mid-loop with a fresh census. The
   re-read-the-row check and the claim-file check exist because presence of
   intent to claim is not the same as having claimed.
2. Discovery of a concurrent editor mid-edit (unknown text appearing in your
   compile output) must stop work immediately and trigger a claims/heartbeats
   sweep before any further write — including "fixing" the file, which is how
   one line of another agent's WIP becomes two agents' incident.
3. When overlapped writes damage a third party's pre-existing code, the
   repair is the minimum restoration of pre-collision content, named and
   dated in-place, after watching the file mtime to confirm no writer is
   mid-flight — never a refactor of the broken region.
4. An honest `live_claims: []` heartbeat is what made this incident
   diagnosable instead of deniable: the schema field did exactly what it was
   designed to do.
