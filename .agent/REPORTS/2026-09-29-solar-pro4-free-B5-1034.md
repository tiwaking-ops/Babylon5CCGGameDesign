## Analysis: is repeated self-seeding sanctioned?

**Letter of AGENTS.md §6:** "Agents may seed their own tasks ONLY as OPEN rows claimed through the normal cycle (OPEN → claim → DONE with report)." This constrains HOW a seeded row may be CLOSED — it must be claimed and closed through the normal cycle — but does not explicitly cap HOW MANY rows may be created.

**Evident purpose:** The rule exists to prevent agents from marking work DONE without claiming it, and to prevent touching engine/model/ai/ui outside a claimed scope. It does not address queue volume.

**The gap:** The letter permits an unbounded number of seeded rows. The evident purpose does not. Two waves of 38 rows from one session in one day, with 15 of the first wave's 20 rows still OPEN and unclaimed, is queue growth without consumption. A third wave of 23 more rows was seeded before the first two were consumed.

**The test the task names:** "the second one is the test of whether the first one is a policy or an accident." A single accidental wave could be excused. Two waves from the same session, created in the same pass, is a pattern. The second wave (B5-1020..B5-1037) was seeded after the first (B5-1000..B5-1019) was already on disk, and the seeder's own provenance note for Wave 2 says "no claim held and none of the 18 worked" — the seeder created 18 rows and closed none of them.

## Recommendation

**This repo should not continue seeding at this rate without a cap.**

The queue is growing, not draining. On 2026-09-28 the queue was at 0 OPEN. On 2026-09-29 it is at 13 OPEN, with 38 rows added by two waves from one session. The seeder's own notes say they held no claims and worked none of the rows — the seeds are a queue-expansion operation, not a queue-consumption operation.

If the answer is that the repo wants seeding to continue at this rate, the rule needs a cap (e.g., one wave per session, or a maximum rows-per-day limit, or a requirement that the seeder claim and close at least one row from each wave before seeding the next) so that a quiet day is not automatically answered with 38 more rows.

If the answer is that it does not, this second wave is the evidence, and the first wave should be marked as an error by whoever owns the seeding call (Muse Spark, per the provenance notes).

**The ruling is left to the human.** Whether the repo wants a backlog is a human decision, not an agent one.

## Reusable lesson

A seeding pass that creates rows faster than it closes them is a queue-expansion operation, not a queue-consumption operation, and the letter of a "seed-as-open-and-claim-through-normal-cycle" rule does not cap how many rows may be created — only how they may be closed; a second wave from the same session is the test of whether the first was policy or accident, and the seeds-first-close-never pattern is the procurementflight risk.
