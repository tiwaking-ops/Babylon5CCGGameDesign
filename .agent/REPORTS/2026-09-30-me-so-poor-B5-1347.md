## Method — deliberately not the B5-1153 method

B5-1153's own methodology note records a **TRIGGER-keyed** census: it read
`CardEffects.java` and grepped the card JSON for `triggerCondition` literals.

This recount uses a structurally different reader:

1. **Record selection keyed on `type`, not on key presence.** Each card file is
   parsed as structured data and aftermath records are selected by
   `type == 'AFTERMATH'`, then the key-presence set is computed separately so the
   two selection strategies can be compared instead of assumed equal.
2. **Coverage derived from the id sets actually declared in code**, read out of
   `CardEffects.java` rather than carried over from B5-1153's table.
3. **Two text cross-checks at the end** so the structural result is pinned against
   a grep reader.

Receipt: `b5ccg/out/b51347/b51347-receipt.txt` (86 lines, 8 sections).

## Divergence 1 — the per-set split is 59 / 58, not 57 / 60

B5-1153's `TOTAL` row reports `57 | 60 | 117`. Measured: **`59 | 58 | 117`**.

Byte-cited cause: the B5-1153 report is internally inconsistent, and its own
per-value columns are the side that agrees with this recount. The premiere column
sums to 59 and the deluxe column to 58 — exactly the measured split — while the
`TOTAL` row asserts 57 / 60. The error is confined to the two `TOTAL` cells; every
per-value count in the same table is correct.

Consequence: none for the trigger census itself (117 is right either way), but any
consumer that splits exposure by expansion set is reading two wrong cells.

## Divergence 2 — "12 values covering 103 records undispatched" is wrong on both numbers

Measured against the code, not against B5-1153's table:

- `CardEffects.applyAftermathEffect` (`CardEffects.java:467`) dispatches **by id**.
  The only two dispatch sets are `AFTERMATH_NEGOTIATED_SURRENDER`
  (`CardEffects.java:432-436`) and `AFTERMATH_DIPLOMATIC_ADVANTAGE`
  (`CardEffects.java:439-443`), holding **4 ids total**, all four confirmed present
  in the data (receipt section 5). So **4 of 117** records are covered.
- Records with no dispatch entry: **113, not 103.**
- Trigger values with at least one undispatched record: **14, not 12.**

The second number moves because **`WON_DIPLOMACY` is only half covered**, which
B5-1153 recorded as `✅ Handled — 4 of 4` while its own dispatched-id row named only
two records. The other two `WON_DIPLOMACY` records are
`aftermath_united_front` and `de_am_united_front`, and neither id appears in
either dispatch set.

Byte-cited reconciliation of the `103`: B5-1153's own uncovered list sums to 101
records once `MILITARY_PARTICIPANT` is correctly netted to 14 after Negotiated
Surrender. Adding the two `united_front` records it missed gives exactly **103** —
so `103` is a real number, but it is "101 plus the two `united_front` records",
which is not the same quantity as the undispatched total it was presented as.
`LOST_DIPLOMACY` is likewise marked only "referenced", not dispatched: all 4 of its
records are undispatched, and it belongs in the uncovered list on the same footing
as the other 13.

## Divergence 3 — the "1 trigger literal, `LOST_DIPLOMACY`" claim is not reproducible as code

B5-1153 states `CardEffects.java` carries "exactly 1 trigger literal, namely
`LOST_DIPLOMACY`". Measured:

- Quoted exact trigger-value literals in `CardEffects.java`: **0**.
- Lines mentioning `LOST_DIPLOMACY` anywhere: **1**, and it is a **comment** —
  `CardEffects.java:556`, `* new vocabulary: DIPLOMACY_PARTICIPANT, WON_DIPLOMACY
  and LOST_DIPLOMACY`.

The mechanism B5-1153 half-describes is real but is a **substring** test, not a
literal: `isDiplomacyAftermath` (`CardEffects.java:559-561`) reads
`card.getTriggerCondition().contains("DIPLOMACY")`. That matches **12** records
across **three** values — `DIPLOMACY_PARTICIPANT` 4, `WON_DIPLOMACY` 4,
`LOST_DIPLOMACY` 4 — not the 4 that a `LOST_DIPLOMACY` literal would select.

Consequence: the B5-1045 agenda ban (`agendaBarsDiplomacyAftermath`,
`CardEffects.java:571-575`) reaches **12** aftermath records, not 4. Reading it as
a single-literal match understates its scope threefold, and would understate it in
the direction of allowing Diplomacy aftercards the rule intends to bar.

## Reusable lesson

A second reader that differs only in *how* it selects records proves much less than
it appears to when the two selection strategies are provably equivalent on the data
— here "has the key" and "is an aftermath record" select the identical 117 records,
so the headline agreement was guaranteed before the recount began. The independent
value came entirely from re-deriving the *derived* quantities (coverage, undispatched
exposure, helper semantics) from source rather than from the predecessor's table.
Recount the conclusions, not the data the first pass already agreed with.
