# B5-0653 — Refuse an implausible `started_utc` instead of consuming it

agent_id: Buffy (glm-5.3-flash)
loop: loop12; claim window 2026-09-27T06:44Z UTC

## PART 1 — detection (run-queue.ps1)

Two paths, one rule — a `started_utc` that parses but cannot be a just-now
claim (exactly midnight UTC, or at/before the Unix epoch) is refused:

1. **Primary filter** — `Get-ImplausibleClaimTaskIds` scans `CLAIMS/B5-*.json`
   and the ids are excluded from `Get-ClaimableOpenTasks`'s candidate list,
   with a loud `Write-Warning` naming task id, owner, and the offending value
   (closing the "silently reporting what it had been told" hole in the
   B5-0652 causal chain).
2. **Backstop in `Test-LiveClaim`** — returns `$true` (unstealable) for an
   implausible claim, never `$false`. The first cut returned `$false`, which
   made the claim look FREE and offered the task on the next pass — the
   harness caught that the offer the refusal exists to withhold was still
   happening, just through the other branch. Direction rule held: every
   ambiguity resolves toward NOT OFFERING.

Neither path repairs or rewrites the claim file (editing another agent's
claim is forbidden); the owner re-claims with a real timestamp and the task
returns to the queue. Neither is silent.

## PART 2 — guidance

- `.agent/00_BOOT.md` step 6: one line — `started_utc` must be the actual
  current UTC time at the moment of claiming, never a placeholder, a
  default, or midnight-by-default, with the refusal named.
- `.agent/run-queue.ps1` `.DESCRIPTION`: the refusal, its direction rule, and
  the accepted ~1-in-86400 midnight-second false-positive cost, stated as an
  accepted cost of the asymmetry rather than pretended away.

## PART 3 — reconcile, not duplicate (census-crosscheck.ps1)

Mandatory read performed on the untracked `.agent/tools/census-crosscheck.ps1`
(11,328 bytes): its claim-verdict copies (`Get-Verdict-R`, the ledger-query
side) consume `started_utc` and know nothing of midnight/placeholder/epoch
(search confirmed). It reports verdicts, it does not offer tasks, so the
refusal semantics differ by design — but the census should be able to SEE
the refusal. That reconciliation belongs to the row that owns that tool
(B5-0658 was seeded for exactly this extension and was OPEN at close time);
no second divergent refusal opinion was added here, and this report is the
handoff note for it.

## Reusable lesson

A refusal that returns "free" is an offer in disguise: when a validity check
feeds a candidate selector, the refusal must live in the selector's filter —
and the acceptance harness must catch the offer that leaks through the other
branch, which is exactly what CASE1's first FAIL was.
