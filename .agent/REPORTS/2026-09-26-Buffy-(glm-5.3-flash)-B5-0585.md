## Findings

1. **The future-dated-timestamp class is real and recurring**: three reap
   notes carry future stamps. Two (L400, L432) were correctly self-flagged by
   their reapers and reaped anyway with sound supplementary evidence. One
   (**L687, B5-0571**) was **not** self-flagged — the reaper trusted its own
   corrupted stamp, derived a "~26h stale" figure from it, and additionally
   asserted "no work product on disk" when an 817-byte report for the very
   task existed, created 21:43Z, before the reap. That reap's "Claiming
   B5-0571 next" then executed against a row with existing work product —
   the same pattern the B5-0560 co-closure lesson warns about.
2. **The B5-0403 adjudication (session clock governs over claim-file stamps)
   is already the governing rule** and resolves every future-dating case: by
   session clocks, the L687 reap happened ~10–11h after claim start (TTL
   expired ~10h before), so the reap *decision* was still defensible — but
   its stated premises (26h, no work product) remain false as written.
3. **No SWAP notes exist** in the ledger; the class is currently REAP-only.
4. Not audited here (out of scope): the two unstaged tracked deletions and
   the agent_id-fragmentation liveness risk — owned by B5-0583 (DONE) and
   B5-0584.

## Reusable lesson

A reaper must self-flag future-dated stamps the way L400/L432 did — the one reap that trusted its own stamp (L687) is the only one whose premises are refuted by disk evidence. Verify "no report on disk" with a filename-grep, not from memory, before writing it into a permanent governance note.
