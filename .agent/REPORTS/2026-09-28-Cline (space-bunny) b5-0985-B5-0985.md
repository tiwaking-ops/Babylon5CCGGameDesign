## Finding 1 — a cleared gate is not a waiting task

**All six rows are now terminal: three `DONE`, three `BLOCKED`.** The cleared
three were each *already worked and closed*; none is waiting to be claimed. The
table measures whether a **precondition** is satisfied, and is not a work queue.

This is the part a careless reader gets wrong. "Gate cleared" is the moment a
blocked row becomes *claimable*; it is not the moment a task is *available*, and
in these three cases the interval between those two moments closed on its own
while the rows were being worked by other agents. A gate table published without
a status column invites the next agent to claim a row that is already `DONE`.


## Finding 2 — a gate can be conjunct, and both conjuncts must be re-measured

B5-0979 carries **two** conditions. Its earlier `BLOCKED` close-out turned on
the second (a live B5-0977 claim on `docs/DECISIONS.md`) while gate one had
already cleared. Both are clear now. A reader who checks the first precondition
and stops will mis-report the row — and would have, this pass, had I not read the
full row text rather than its seed summary.

## Finding 3 — B5-0481 is a standing trap for scope-wording gates

B5-0481's claim file names `docs/DECISIONS.md` in its `scope` array. A naive
directory scan therefore reports DECISIONS.md as contended, which would keep
B5-0979's second gate shut. `ledger-query.ps1` reads its row `DONE` with verdict
`STALE` at 1245.4 min — it is not live.

**A claim's `scope` string is a declaration; liveness is the three-signal
verdict.** A gate phrased against a scope must be evaluated against the verdict.
This is the B5-0609 family again: reading a declared or absent value as a
positive fact about the world.

## Finding 4 — B5-0953 remains unstealable, and is why half the gates are shut

`started_utc` reads `2026-09-29T07:04:00Z` against a wall clock of
`2026-09-28T20:45Z` — about 619 minutes in the **future**. A future-dated claim
has negative age, and negative compares as younger than any TTL, so it reads
`LIVE` forever and never ages out. `run-queue.ps1 -DryRun` therefore refuses to
offer B5-0953 under the B5-0653 guard:

```

owner [solar-pro4] parses and is 619.4 minutes AHEAD of the wall clock, beyond the
60-minute tolerance. ... Not repaired here: a claim file belongs to its owner.
```

B5-0969 and B5-0983 are both gated on that claim being released, so both stay
shut for as long as it exists. Repairing or reaping a foreign claim is outside
