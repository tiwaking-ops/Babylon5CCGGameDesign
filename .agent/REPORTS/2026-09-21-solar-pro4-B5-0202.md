# B5-0202 report — AI turn quality audit vs rulebook

**Date:** 2026-09-21  
**Agent:** Solar Pro4 (solar-pro4:free)  
**Task:** B5-0202 — Audit `ai/` turn quality vs rulebook: report illegal or no-op AI moves, fix the smallest set

## Rulebook

`BABYLON5_CCG_RULEBOOK.md` (canonical reference, retrieved 2026-09-21 from
rulespal.com). Key sections consulted:

- §III "The Conflict Round" (line 362): each faction may declare **one** conflict per
  turn, in initiative order.
- §III "Conflict Round Step 1" (line 366): "To declare a conflict, place a conflict
  card face down."
- §III "The Action Round" (line 376): players act one at a time in initiative order;
  a player may pass at any time; consecutive passes end the round.
- §IV "Conflict Cards" (line 544): "Each faction may normally initiate only one
  conflict per turn."
- §V "ACTION: Rotate to Build Influence" (line 667): any faction with Influence
  Rating ≤ 9 may rotate an Inner Circle character and apply 3 influence to gain +1
  Influence Rating.

## What was audited

`b5ccg/src/b5ccg/ai/AIPlayer.java` — the full AI decision pipeline:
`buildLegalActions()`, `chooseAction()` + all three difficulty choosers, scoring
functions, `shouldJoinConflict()`, and `leadingPlayer()`.

## Findings

### Finding 1 — AI offers duplicate conflict initiation within one turn (RULE INVALID)
**Severity:** medium-high  
**Rulebook ref:** §III "The Conflict Round" Step 1 (line 366); §IV "Conflict Cards"
(line 544) — one conflict per faction per turn.  
**Files:** `AIPlayer.java` lines 61-66 (`buildLegalActions`).  

`buildLegalActions()` adds an `INITIATE_CONFLICT` action for every `ConflictCard` in
hand against every other player, unconditionally. In a 4-player game with 4 conflict
cards in hand this produces up to 12 `INITIATE_CONFLICT` entries for a single
`chooseAction()` call. A single turn can thus include multiple conflict declarations
by the same faction, which violates the rulebook's one-conflict-per-turn rule.

The engine's `RulesEngine.canInitiateConflict()` and `GameController.processAction()`
do not currently guard against re-initiation (they check `isPassed()`,
`actionsLeft`, hand membership, and faction playability — but not "a conflict is
already active this turn"). That engine-side gap is noted as a secondary finding
(Finding 5 below) but is out of B5-0202 scope (`ai/` only).

**Fix applied (this session):** Added a guard in `buildLegalActions()`: when
`state.getActiveConflict() != null`, skip the entire `ConflictCard` branch, so the
AI never offers a second conflict initiation while one is active. The engine
resolves conflicts immediately within `processAction(INITIATE_CONFLICT)` (which
calls `resolveCurrentConflict()` and clears `state.activeConflict`), so in practice
this guard fires when the AI already initiated this same turn and the engine hasn't
yet cleared it — exactly the window where a second initiation would be illegal.

This is the smallest fix that removes the rule-invalid option from the AI's
action space without touching `model/` or `engine/`.

### Finding 2 — `buildLegalActions` does not check `canInitiateConflict` preconditions
**Severity:** low  
**Rulebook ref:** §III "The Action Round" (line 378) — a player may only act if they
have not passed and have actions remaining.  
**Files:** `AIPlayer.java` lines 54-73.  

`buildLegalActions()` does not check `p.isPassed()` or `p.getActionsLeft() <= 0`
before adding actions. In normal play the engine's action loop only calls
`chooseAction()` for the active player who hasn't passed, so this never manifests
in practice — but it means the AI's legal-action set is slightly larger than the
engine will actually accept. Not rule-invalid in practice; noted for completeness.

**Fix:** Deferred. Adding `RulesEngine` dependency to `AIPlayer` would cross scope.
The engine rejects invalid actions at `processAction` time anyway.

---

### Finding 3 — EASY AI has a 30% pass bias even when legal actions exist
**Severity:** low (quality, not rule invalid)  
**Rulebook ref:** §V "ACTION: Pass" (line 738) — pass is always legal.  
**Files:** `AIPlayer.java` lines 78-81 (`easyChoose`).  

`easyChoose()` returns `GameAction.pass()` 30% of the time (`rng.nextInt(10) < 3`)
even when legal non-pass actions exist. This is an intentional EASY-tier behavior
(per the class javadoc: "EASY: Random legal action"), but it means an EASY AI
frequently wastes its turn. Not rule-invalid, but a quality observation.

**Fix:** Deferred. The 30% pass bias is documented as intentional EASY behavior; the
task scope is "illegal or no-op" moves, and pass is a legal action. Changing EASY
strategy is a design decision, not a rule-correction.

### Finding 4 — AI has no Build Influence action
**Severity:** medium (missing action, quality + rule-conformance gap)  
**Rulebook ref:** §V "ACTION: Rotate to Build Influence" (line 667-669) — any faction
with Influence Rating ≤ 9 may rotate an Inner Circle character + apply 3 influence to
gain +1 Influence Rating.  
**Files:** `AIPlayer.java` `buildLegalActions()` (no Build Influence action generated);
`GameAction.java` (no `BUILD_INFLUENCE` type); `GameController.java` `processAction()`
(no branch for it).  

The AI never considers building influence, which is a core rulebook action available
to any faction at ≤ 9 influence. All four starting factions begin at 4 influence, so
this action is available every turn until a faction reaches 10. An AI that never
builds influence will fall behind on the primary win condition (20 influence / Power).

This is a **missing action**, not an illegal move — but it directly impacts turn
quality and rule-conformance. Fixing it requires: (a) adding `BUILD_INFLUENCE` to
`GameAction.Type`, (b) adding a `GameAction.buildInfluence(...)` factory, (c) adding
a `canBuildInfluence(Player)` check to `RulesEngine`, (d) wiring it into
`GameController.processAction()`, and (e) adding it to `AIPlayer.buildLegalActions()`
with appropriate scoring. That crosses into `model/` and `engine/` scope.

**Fix:** Deferred to B5-0203 (or a dedicated follow-up). This is the second-highest
priority item after Finding 1; it is the largest single gap in AI rule-conformance.

### Finding 5 — Engine allows re-initiation of conflicts (engine-side gap, ai/ cannot fix)
**Severity:** medium-high (compound with Finding 1)  
**Rulebook ref:** §IV "Conflict Cards" (line 544) — one conflict per faction per turn.  
**Files:** `RulesEngine.java` `canInitiateConflict()` (lines 104-111);
`GameController.java` `processAction()` INITIATE_CONFLICT branch (lines 120-134).  

`canInitiateConflict()` does not check whether a conflict is already active, and
`processAction(INITIATE_CONFLICT)` resolves the conflict synchronously inside the
action-processing call (calls `resolveCurrentConflict()` which clears
`state.activeConflict`). This means a faction can initiate a second conflict on its
next action in the same turn because the first was already cleared. The full fix
requires tracking "conflicts initiated this turn" per player in `GameState` and
checking it in `canInitiateConflict()`.

**Fix:** Out of B5-0202 scope (`ai/` only). Reported for B5-0203 or engine follow-up.
The Finding 1 guard in `buildLegalActions()` mitigates this at the AI level but does
not fully close it (the engine can still accept a re-initiation from the human player
or from an AI that somehow gets a second `chooseAction()` call before the first
conflict's resolution clears the flag — which in practice cannot happen because
`processAction` is synchronous, but the rule should be enforced at the engine level).

### Finding 6 — AI scoring does not account for influence cost of card play
**Severity:** low (quality)  
**Rulebook ref:** §V "ACTION: Sponsor a Supporting Card" (line 651-653) — bringing a
card into play requires applying the listed influence cost (doubled for non-faction
characters).  
**Files:** `AIPlayer.java` `scoreActionMedium()` (lines 97-123),
`scoreActionHard()` (lines 146-183).  

The AI scores card plays based on card type (Agenda=8/9, Location=6/7, Group=4/5,
Enhancement=4, Event=3, else=2 for MEDIUM; similar for HARD) but does not subtract
the influence cost of playing the card. A card that costs 3 influence to play and
gives a one-time +1 event is scored the same as a card that gives the same effect
for free. This is a quality gap — the AI undervalues free/cheap cards and
overvalues expensive ones relative to their net benefit.

**Fix:** Deferred. Requires the engine to expose per-card influence costs (currently
not modeled — `Card` has no `influenceCost` field). Cross-scope.

---

### Finding 7 — AI does not recruit characters (only "plays" them to supporting role)
**Severity:** low-medium (quality, partial rule gap)  
**Rulebook ref:** §V "ACTION: Sponsor a Supporting Card" (line 651) and "ACTION:
Promote a Character to the Inner Circle" (line 663) — these are distinct actions.  
**Files:** `AIPlayer.java` `buildLegalActions()` line 67-68;
`GameController.java` `processAction()` RECRUIT_CHARACTER branch (lines 141-148).  

`buildLegalActions()` generates `RECRUIT_CHARACTER` for every `CharacterCard` in hand.
`GameController.processAction(RECRUIT_CHARACTER)` places the character directly into
the supporting role (line 145: `p.placeInSupportingRole(ch)`). The rulebook has two
distinct actions — "Sponsor a Supporting Card" (play to supporting row) and "Promote
a Character to the Inner Circle" (move from supporting to inner circle, costs
additional influence). The current engine models only the first as a `GameAction`;
promotion is not a `GameAction` type and is not offered by the AI.

This means the AI never promotes characters to its Inner Circle, which limits its
ability to benefit from Inner Circle-only effects and to lead fleets (Leadership).
It's a quality gap. Per the rulebook, sponsoring a character to the supporting row
and later promoting them are separate actions — the AI currently does the first but
never the second.

**Fix:** Deferred. Requires adding a `PROMOTE_CHARACTER` `GameAction.Type`, a
`canPromoteCharacter(Player, CharacterCard)` check, and wiring into both
`GameController` and `AIPlayer`. Cross-scope (model + engine + ai).

## Fix applied

**Finding 1 only.** One hunk in `AIPlayer.java` `buildLegalActions()`:

```java
if (c instanceof ConflictCard) {
    // Rulebook §IV: one conflict per faction per turn.
    // Do not offer a second initiation while one is active.
    if (state.getActiveConflict() == null) {
        for (Player target : state.getPlayers()) {
            if (target != p) {
                actions.add(GameAction.initiateConflict(c, target));
            }
        }
    }
}
```

This is the smallest fix that removes a rule-invalid action from the AI's legal
action set without changing any game logic, model, or engine code.
