## Instrument correction, self-caught and disclosed

The first battery run (v1, 56 lines, discarded) had two flaws I caught *before
trusting it*: `rc=$?` after `out=$(java … | tail -1)` captured **tail's** exit
code, not java's (so v1's exit column was meaningless — always 0 regardless of the
JVM), and the captured final line was the `Picked up JAVA_TOOL_OPTIONS` noise line
rather than the verdict. The second battery (v2, authoritative) captures java's
exit directly and filters the noise line. v1 is disclosed here rather than deleted
silently; v2's numbers below are the record.

## Verdict against the row

"Report any class that exceeds 1 second or exits non-zero on the current green
tree" — reported: three classes exceeded 1 s (table above, with the
load-variance attribution); zero classes exited non-zero. The B5-0956 runtime and
exit-contract claims are **confirmed for exits, corrected for timing**: 8/8 green

refuted as a ceiling.

## Reusable lesson

An exit contract and a timing claim are different kinds of assertion: the exit
contract is testable to zero flakes across 56 runs, the timing claim is a
single-session observation that host load refutes on demand — and a comment that
states both as one fact invites the next reader to assert the flaky half.
