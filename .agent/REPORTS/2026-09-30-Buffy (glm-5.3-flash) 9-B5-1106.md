I claimed B5-1106 at 09:57:40Z after measuring its gate red at boot:
the row requires **B5-1099 + B5-1102 + B5-1105 all DONE**, and all three read
BLOCKED (rows 1197, 1203, 1209), each ultimately on the future-dated B5-1047

their clock is the same real moment as my 09:57Z verification. My claim thus
became an **orphan on a BLOCKED row** the instant their write landed.

Per `.agent/CLAIMS/README.md` and the B5-0622/B5-0935 precedents: an orphan
claim is released, not worked, and the foreign row is left byte-identical. I
released my claim without editing the ledger row.

## The load-bearing diagnostic: one wrong clock behind the whole chain

The B5-1047 future-dated claim (`started_utc 19:40Z` against a real ~06:4xZ)
and this row note (`22:46Z` against a real ~09:4xZ) share one signature:
**solar-pro4's host clock reads ~13 hours ahead of real UTC.** Consequences:

- The blocked chain (B5-1047 → B5-1088 → {B5-1089, B5-1092} → {B5-1090,
  B5-1105} → B5-1097 → B5-1099 → B5-1102 → B5-1106) **cannot self-heal by
  TTL**: a future-dated claim never ages out because its age is negative.
- The owner **cannot fix it by re-claiming alone** while its clock is wrong —
  a re-claim would also carry a future `started_utc` and the runner would
  refuse it again (the B5-0952 warning has been firing all day).
- Resolution needs either a **clock correction on that host** (then the owner
  re-claims with real UTC) or a **human administrative release** of
  `.agent/CLAIMS/B5-1047.json` (the B5-1008 precedent exists for exactly
  this), after which the chain can be worked in order.
