# B5-0422 — Pass-bias cascade design proposal

*`author_llm: Solar Pro4 (solar-pro4:free)`*

## Context

The B5-0409 verdict (solar-pro4:free, 2026-09-25) established that the multi-round stall is an EASY pass-bias cascade inside the B5-0351 0.35–0.70 band, NOT a per-player actionsLeft cap (no such cap exists; D6 runs until all players pass) and NOT a B5-0321 seat gap (canPromote/executePromote are implemented; zero promotions is a pass-bias consequence). EASY's designed target is ~53% pass bias; observed 0.523 (B5-0406) and 0.567 (B5-0409). MEDIUM/HARD are deterministic and cost-aware.

The cascade: EASY passes ~53% of the time; when most/all players are EASY, most action cycles are pass cycles; the round advances by draw-phase card draws and turn rotation but very few actions fire; influence grows slowly; games terminate naturally at Rating 20 after ~12 rounds / ~118s (B5-0409 no-timeout probe) but the harness's 60s window labels them "stalled." The stall is a labeling artifact on natural termination, but the underlying design question stands: is a 53% per-cycle pass rate on EASY the right default, given that most experimental decks are EASY-heavy?

This proposal is **proposal-only — no src/ or resources/ edits**. It is not a stealth retune: EASY pass bias was a deliberate design decision (B5-0202c Finding 3), and B5-0413 already closed the harness log-parser counter only.

## Option A — Retune EASY pass-bias band downward

**Description**: lower the EASY pass-bias floor from 0.35 to something like 0.15–0.25, keeping the 0.70 ceiling, so EASY passes less often and fires more actions per cycle. The contract test band becomes 0.15–0.70 (or 0.20–0.70).

**Mechanism**: in `AIPlayer.easyChoose` (or wherever the pass-bias coin is flipped), replace the 0.35 floor with the new floor. The MEDIUM/HARD paths are untouched — they are deterministic and already cost-aware.

**Effect on B5-0351 contract bands**: the contract asserts a band, not a point. Lowering the floor to 0.15 widens the band from [0.35, 0.70] (width 0.35) to [0.15, 0.70] (width 0.55). The observed rate would shift down from ~0.53 toward ~0.30–0.40 (rough linear interpolation; actual rate depends on the legal-action distribution in the fixture, which already skews toward pass-able states). The contract still passes as long as the observed rate stays inside the new band — and a 0.30 rate is inside [0.15, 0.70]. So the contract is **not endangered** by this change; it actually becomes easier to satisfy because the band is wider.

**Effect on games**: more actions per cycle on EASY decks; faster influence growth; fewer harness "stall" labels; games still terminate naturally (Rating 20 reachable faster). MEDIUM/HARD unchanged.

**Risk**: a lower EASY floor makes EASY less "easy" — it fires more actions, some of which may be poor (EASY is random over the legal set, not scored). More actions ≠ better actions. Could increase conflict volume and make EASY-vs-EASY games noisier. Also narrows the behavioral gap between EASY and MEDIUM (MEDIUM already fires greedily; if EASY fires more, the tier distinction blurs).

**Recommendation strength**: medium. Addresses the symptom (too many passes) without touching the cascade structure.

## Option B — Change harness and Main default seat mix

**Description**: change the default seat assignment in the harness runner and/or Main so that not all four seats are EASY by default. E.g., default to [EASY, MEDIUM, HARD, EASY] or [MEDIUM, MEDIUM, EASY, EASY] instead of [EASY, EASY, EASY, EASY].

**Mechanism**: in `HeadlessMultiRoundTest` (and/or `Main`), change the difficulty array passed to the player constructor. No AI logic change.

**Effect on B5-0351 contract bands**: **none**. B5-0351 tests each tier in isolation over 50 identical states per tier; seat mix does not enter the contract. The contract is unaffected and continues to pass unchanged.

**Effect on games**: mixed-tier games fire more actions because MEDIUM/HARD rarely pass (they are cost-aware and deterministic; they pass only when no legal action is net-positive). The cascade is diluted because at least one or two seats are action-prone. Harness "stall" labels drop because games progress faster. But this only affects the default harness/Main invocation — a user (or another harness) can still request all-EASY.

**Risk**: changes the default experimental baseline. Any downstream probe that assumes all-EASY default (including B5-0408/B5-0409-style multi-round probes) would need to re-baseline. Also does not address the underlying design question of whether EASY's 53% pass rate is right — it sidesteps it by changing the seat mix.

**Recommendation strength**: high as a short-term harness-default improvement; low as a design answer. It is the cheapest change (no AI logic, no contract impact, no retune) and directly reduces the harness "stall" artifact.

## Option C — Add un-pass incentives to EASY

**Description**: give EASY a small incentive to avoid passing when a low-cost action is available — e.g., a tiny scoring nudge that makes a zero-cost action slightly preferred over pass, without turning EASY into MEDIUM. The intent is to reduce pass rate without changing the coin-flip bias or the tier distinction.

**Mechanism**: in `AIPlayer.easyChoose`, when the legal set contains a zero-cost (or very-low-cost) action, bias the random pick toward that action instead of pure uniform. Could be as simple as: if any legal action has cost ≤ 0, restrict the EASY random pick to the zero-cost subset with some probability p (e.g., 0.5), else uniform over all legal.

**Effect on B5-0351 contract bands**: **endangered unless carefully scoped**. B5-0351 check 4 asserts "EASY spreads picks across equal-value plays (ev0 65, ev1 65 of 300)" — a uniform spread over equal-value plays. If EASY is biased toward zero-cost actions, the spread check could fail because zero-cost events would be picked more often than equal-cost alternatives. The contract would need a new check or a reworded spread check. The pass-bias check (check 3, band 0.35–0.70) would see the rate drop, possibly below 0.35 if the incentive is strong — which would fail the contract. So this option **requires a contract update** to be safe.

**Effect on games**: fewer passes on EASY when cheap actions exist; more actions fired; faster games. But EASY still passes when all legal actions have nonzero cost (the incentive only fires on zero-cost). The cascade is reduced but not eliminated — EASY still passes when the legal set is all-costly.

**Risk**: contract churn (B5-0351 checks 3 and 4 both potentially affected). Behavioral change to EASY that is subtle and hard to characterize — "EASY with a slight zero-cost nudge" is a new tier behavior that needs its own contract, not a tweak to the existing one.

**Recommendation strength**: low. The contract impact is disproportionate to the benefit, and the behavioral change is hard to specify cleanly.

## Option D — MEDIUM/HARD termination safety

**Description**: add a safety net so MEDIUM/HARD cannot loop indefinitely even if they somehow keep finding net-positive actions — e.g., a per-player action cap per round, or a diminishing-returns score clamp that forces pass after N consecutive net-positive actions. This is a termination guarantee, not a pass-bias change.

**Mechanism**: in the controller loop or in AIPlayer, track actions taken by this player this round; after N (e.g., 3–5) actions, force pass regardless of score. Or add a score clamp: after N consecutive net-positive picks, the next pick's score is clamped to ≤ 0 so pass wins.

**Effect on B5-0351 contract bands**: **potential conflict**. B5-0351 does not currently test per-player action caps (the contract tests are per-choice, over isolated states, not multi-action sequences). A per-player cap would not directly fail the existing 10 checks because they don't exercise multi-action rounds. But it would be a new behavioral constraint that MEDIUM/HARD probes would need to account for. The zero-cost invariance checks (5, 8) would still pass because the cap doesn't change which of two equal-cost cards is picked first.

**Effect on games**: MEDIUM/HARD cannot dominate a round by firing many actions — they are capped. This is a fairness/interaction constraint more than a cascade fix. It does not reduce EASY passes (EASY is already the pass source). It prevents the inverse problem (one player firing too many actions) but does not fix the cascade.

**Risk**: a per-player action cap is a new rule that needs rulebook alignment. The B5-0409 verdict explicitly said "no per-player actionsLeft cap exists" and called the idea STALE — adding one now would be a rulebook change, not just an AI tweak. Also does not address the EASY pass-bias cascade that is the actual problem.

**Recommendation strength**: low as a cascade fix (wrong target); medium as a separate fairness proposal (but that's out of B5-0422 scope, which is about the cascade).

## Comparative summary

| Option | Target | Contract impact | Game effect | Cost | Addresses cascade root cause? |
|--------|--------|-----------------|-------------|------|-------------------------------|
| A — retune EASY band | EASY pass bias | Low (wider band, still passes) | More EASY actions, noisier EASY games, blurs EASY/MEDIUM gap | Small (one constant) | Partially (reduces passes) |
| B — seat mix default | Harness/Main default | None (contract is per-tier isolated) | Dilutes cascade via mixed tiers, fastest stall reduction | Smallest (array change) | No (sidesteps, doesn't fix EASY) |
| C — un-pass incentive | EASY zero-cost nudge | High (checks 3+4 need reword) | Fewer passes when cheap actions exist, incomplete (still passes on costly) | Medium (contract + logic) | Partially (only on zero-cost) |
| D — MEDIUM/HARD cap | Termination safety | Low (contract not exercised) but rulebook implications | Fairness constraint, not a cascade fix | Medium (new rule) | No (wrong target) |

## Recommendation

**Option B first (seat-mix default), then Option A (EASY band retune) if the design decision is made to retune EASY.**

Rationale:
- Option B is the cheapest, lowest-risk change that directly reduces the harness "stall" artifact. It does not retune AI behavior, does not touch the B5-0351 contract, and does not silently change the experimental baseline for anyone who opts into all-EASY. It is a harness/Main default change only.
- Option A is the only option that actually changes EASY's pass bias (the root cause) without high contract churn or subtle behavioral ambiguity. The band widens rather than narrows, so the contract is safer, not more fragile. The main cost is a deliberate design decision: is 53% pass bias on EASY still the right target, or should it be lower (e.g., 30–40%)? That decision belongs to the human, not to this proposal — this proposal only spells out the contract and game effects.
- Options C and D are not recommended for B5-0422. C's contract churn is disproportionate; D targets the wrong problem (cascade is EASY-pass-driven, not MEDIUM/HARD-overaction-driven) and reopens the "actionsLeft cap" question that B5-0409 already closed as STALE.

**Honesty notes**:
- This proposal does not change EASY pass bias. It documents options and effects. Any retune (Option A or C) requires a human go/no-go on the new pass-bias target and a contract update to B5-0351.
- The stall itself is mostly a harness labeling artifact (60s window vs. natural ~118s termination at Rating 20 per B5-0409 no-timeout probe). The design question is whether the underlying 53% EASY pass rate is desirable for the intended audience, not whether games are actually stuck.
- The B5-0409 verdict stands: stall is an EASY pass-bias cascade inside the B5-0351 band, not a cap and not a seat gap. This proposal is consistent with that verdict.
